Work together on one law at a time.

The Intersectionist Party finds specific changes that people across political lines can agree on, then helps them work together to pass, revise, or repeal a law or regulation.

How it works

Why we’re forming

Political parties are increasingly polarized, leaving people in the center with less say in what gets done. We want to give them more influence by finding specific proposals that draw broad agreement across political lines.

People who usually vote differently can work together on an issue they agree on while keeping their own views on everything else.

How it works

Define the change

Spell out what would change, who would be affected, and what it would cost. Give everyone the same proposal to consider.

Measure agreement

Ask a representative sample of the people affected. Publish the exact question, who was asked, how support was counted, and significant uncertainties or objections.

Build a coalition

Bring people together around the proposal. Take it to the lawmakers or regulators who can act on it, then check whether the change delivered its expected benefit.

How we use GPT

We use GPT to draft questions, simulate possible viewpoints, and help organize feedback. When we ask for opinions, we’ll disclose any role GPT plays in asking questions or summarizing answers.

We label GPT-generated samples as simulations and report them separately from responses people gave. Any percentage we report as measured public support comes from people’s responses.

What a proposal looks like

Suppose a community wants to make local drinking-water test results easier to find. The proposal would name who must publish them, how often, in what format, and at what cost.
